# Security Best Practices

## Building a Security-First Culture
- Security is not a one-time event - it is a daily habit
- Small actions compound into strong protection
- This chapter covers practical habits for your daily work
- These are easy to adopt and make a real difference

---

## Clean Desk Policy

## Why It Matters
- Sensitive documents left on desks can be seen or stolen
- Unlocked screens expose company data to anyone walking by
- Visitors, cleaning staff, and even colleagues may see things they should not

## The Rules
- Lock your screen every time you leave your desk (`Win+L` or `Ctrl+Cmd+Q`)
- Put away sensitive documents in locked drawers
- Shred documents you no longer need
- Do not leave sticky notes with passwords on your monitor
- Clear whiteboards after meetings

## Physical Security

## Protecting the Physical Space
- Do not let strangers tailgate through secure doors
- Politely challenge unfamiliar faces in restricted areas
- Wear your badge visibly at all times
- Report lost or stolen badges immediately
- Do not prop open secure doors
- Escort visitors at all times

## Social Media Security

## What Attackers Learn from Your Posts
- Where you work and what you do
- When you are traveling (home is empty)
- Names of colleagues and managers
- Technologies and tools your company uses
- Details for crafting targeted phishing attacks

---

## Social Media Guidelines

- **Never post** confidential company information
- **Do not share** photos of your screen, badge, or office interior
- **Be cautious** about listing your exact role and responsibilities
- **Review** your privacy settings on all platforms
- **Think twice** before posting about work projects
- **Do not accept** connection requests from people you do not know
- **Separate** personal and professional social media where possible

---

## What Not to Post

| Seems Harmless | Why It Is Risky |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Photo at your desk | Badge, screen content, or documents visible |
| "Excited to start using [new software]!" | Tells attackers what to target |
| "Working late, office is empty" | Physical security risk |
| Vacation photos in real time | Tells people you are away |
| "My manager [Name] approved the deal" | Spear phishing material |

## Software and Update Security

- Install updates promptly when notified
- Updates patch known security vulnerabilities
- Enable automatic updates where possible
- Only install software approved by IT
- Do not download cracked or pirated software
- Uninstall software you no longer use

## Secure Printing

- Collect printouts immediately - do not leave them in the tray
- Use secure print / pull printing if available
- Shred sensitive printouts after use
- Do not print sensitive documents on shared printers in open areas
- Consider whether you really need to print at all

## Third-Party Vendor Security

## Supply Chain Risk
- Your organization is only as secure as its weakest partner
- Vendors with access to your systems or data are a risk
- Major breaches have occurred through vendor access

## Your Role
- Only grant vendors the minimum access they need
- Ensure vendors follow your company's security policies
- Report if a vendor asks for unusual access
- Revoke vendor access as soon as the project ends

---

## Third-Party Risk Examples

| Incident | What Happened |
|----------|---------------|
| Target (2013) | Attacker compromised an HVAC vendor's credentials |
| SolarWinds (2020) | Malicious code inserted into a trusted software update |
| Kaseya (2021) | Ransomware delivered through IT management software |

- Always verify vendor security practices
- Do not assume a vendor is secure because they are well known

## Travel Security

## Before You Travel
- Back up your data before leaving
- Only bring devices you actually need
- Enable device encryption and remote wipe
- Know how to contact IT security from abroad

## During Travel
- Never leave devices unattended
- Use a `VPN` for all connections
- Be cautious of public charging stations ("juice jacking")
- Use a privacy screen on planes and in public
- Disable Bluetooth and Wi-Fi when not in use

---

## AI and Chatbot Security

## New Risks from AI Tools
- Do not paste confidential data into public AI chatbots
- Do not upload company documents to external AI services
- AI-generated phishing emails are more convincing than ever
- Deepfake audio and video can impersonate colleagues

## Guidelines
- Use only company-approved AI tools
- Treat AI output with the same caution as any external source
- Verify any AI-generated instructions through official channels
- Report deepfake attempts to security

## Security Awareness for Daily Habits

## The Security Mindset Checklist

- Before clicking a link: "Is this expected? Is the source trustworthy?"
- Before sharing data: "Does this person need this? Am I using a secure channel?"
- Before installing software: "Is this approved? Is the source legitimate?"
- Before leaving your desk: "Is my screen locked? Are documents secured?"
- Before posting online: "Could this help an attacker?"
