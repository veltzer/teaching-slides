# Review Culture

---
## What This Chapter Covers

- Psychological safety
- Blameless feedback
- Mentoring through reviews
- Setting team norms
- Building habits that last
- Repairing a broken culture

---
## What Culture Means Here

- The unwritten rules of how reviews happen
- Who feels safe asking questions
- Whether disagreement is welcomed
- What gets celebrated, what gets dismissed
- More important than the tooling

---
## Healthy Culture Practices

![healthy_culture](svg/courses/development_methodologies/code-review-best-practices/13_review_culture/healthy_culture.svg)

---
## Psychological Safety

- A team where people feel safe to be wrong
- Junior devs ask questions; senior devs admit ignorance
- Mistakes are debugged, not blamed
- Review comments aren't personal attacks
- Without it, reviews degrade fast

---
## Building Safety

- Senior people demonstrate vulnerability ("I don't know", "I was wrong")
- Praise good work in public
- Criticise behaviour, not character
- Address bad behaviour quickly
- Manager sets the tone

---
## Blameless Feedback

- "What went wrong?" not "who screwed up?"
- A review comment about code, not coder
- A bug is a system problem, not an individual one
- Post-mortems are about prevention, not punishment
- Same culture in reviews

---
## Mentoring Through Reviews

- Senior reviewers teach in PR comments
- "Have you considered X because Y?" plants seeds
- Pattern: comment + brief explanation + link
- Junior dev learns; senior dev codifies their knowledge
- Compound interest of mentorship

---
## Cross-Team Reviews

- Reviewer outside your area
- Surfaces cross-cutting concerns
- Spreads patterns across teams
- Builds informal networks
- Common at companies that scale well

---
## Setting Team Norms

- A short doc: "How we review at <team>"
- Includes: tone, response time, severity vocabulary, who reviews what
- Living document
- Onboarding includes reading it
- Review meetings include "how we review" check-in

---
## Sample Norms

- We respond to review requests within one business day
- We use "nit:" for non-blocking style suggestions
- We praise good work when we see it
- We address comments by either changing or replying
- We don't approve PRs we haven't read

---
## Habits That Compound

- Review with your morning coffee, not at end of day
- Self-review before requesting reviewers
- Praise specific things, not just "looks good"
- Ask before insisting
- Small habits make large culture

---
## Demoting Heroics

- "Sarah saved the production deploy by reviewing at midnight"
- Sounds praiseworthy, signals dysfunction
- Why was it midnight? Why was Sarah alone?
- Culture should make heroics rare, not celebrated
- Sustainable practices > rescue stories

---
## Repairing a Broken Culture

- Acknowledge the problem
- Pick one or two changes; not everything at once
- Lead by example
- Measure, but don't weaponise
- Patience: culture changes over months, not weeks

---
## Manager's Role

- Doesn't review every PR (usually shouldn't)
- Notices patterns (queue length, comment tone, blocking PRs)
- Coaches one-on-one
- Adjusts process when patterns persist
- Removes obstacles for reviewers

---
## When Culture Goes Wrong

- Brilliant jerk who tears apart every PR
- Manager who scoldings authors for review delays
- "Move fast" rhetoric used to skip reviews
- Approval shopping
- Each erodes a different value; address each

---
## Recognising Reviewers

- Public credit for thorough reviews
- Time spent on reviews counted in performance
- Reviewers treated as partners, not gatekeepers
- "We shipped this together" attribution
- The team that recognises reviewers gets better reviews

---
## Long-Term Indicators

- Junior devs become senior devs through reviews
- Bus factor improves over years
- Codebase becomes more consistent
- Bug rates decline
- Onboarding gets easier
- All visible in retrospect; subtle quarter to quarter

---
## Common Cultural Mistakes

- Tolerating one toxic reviewer
- Using reviews for ranking
- Skipping reviews for "important" people
- Letting the loudest voice always win
- Forgetting that culture is what you reward
