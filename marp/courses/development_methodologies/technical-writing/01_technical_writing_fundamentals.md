# Technical Writing Fundamentals

---
## What This Chapter Covers

- Why technical writing matters
- The cost of bad documentation
- What makes writing "good"
- The writing process at a high level
- Why engineers should care
- Setting expectations for the course

---
## Why It Matters

- Code is read 10x more than written; docs even more
- A great library with bad docs is unused
- A mediocre library with great docs spreads
- Developer time is the bottleneck; docs are the multiplier
- Writing is one of the most leveraged skills in software

---
## The Cost of Bad Documentation

- Repeated questions in chat that the doc could have answered
- Onboarding times measured in months instead of weeks
- Decisions revisited because no one remembered the rationale
- Customer support burden when users can't self-serve
- Real money, every day, in every team that ships software

---
## Characteristics of Good Technical Writing

- **Clear**: a reader's first reading is the right reading
- **Concise**: every word earns its place
- **Correct**: the code examples actually work
- **Complete**: covers what the user needs without padding
- **Current**: reflects the code as it is now, not as it was

---
## The Writing Process

- **Plan**: who, what, why
- **Draft**: get something down quickly
- **Revise**: cut, restructure, clarify
- **Edit**: tighten language, fix typos
- **Review**: someone else reads
- Skipping any step shows in the finished writing

---
## Why Engineers Should Care

- Engineers write the most technical content
- Engineers know the system best
- Outsourcing docs to writers loses accuracy
- Writing well is a force multiplier on technical work
- Promoted engineers tend to write well; correlation is causation

---
## Writing Is Thinking

- The act of writing surfaces gaps in your understanding
- "I'll explain this in the doc" reveals what you don't actually know
- Writing first; coding second is sometimes the right order
- Especially for designs: write the doc, then build it
- Many bugs are caught during documentation

---
## What Makes Documentation Hard

- Writing for someone who knows less than you
- Anticipating questions you've forgotten you ever had
- Keeping it current as the code changes
- Balancing depth and brevity
- Time-pressured engineers cut docs first

## What This Course Won't Do

- Make you a novelist or essayist
- Replace formal English instruction
- Cover every documentation tool in detail
- Argue for one writing tool over another
- This is *technical* writing — utilitarian by design

---

- Teach the principles that work across formats
- Cover the document types engineers actually write
- Cover the tools the industry uses
- Practical advice you can apply tomorrow
- Habits that compound over a career

---
## Common Beginner Mistakes

- Writing for yourself instead of the reader
- Including everything you know, not what the reader needs
- Long sentences with passive voice
- "It is recommended that the user..." instead of "do this"
- Treating docs as a one-time deliverable

---
## A Useful Mindset

- Imagine the reader: tired, in a hurry, doesn't speak your jargon
- Write for *them*, not for yourself
- If they get lost, that's *your* fault, not theirs
- Writing well is a service to your readers
- Good service compounds; bad service drives them away
