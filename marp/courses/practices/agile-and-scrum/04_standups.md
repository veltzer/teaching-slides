## What the Daily Scrum Is

- A 15-minute event for the Developers, every working day
- Same time, same place
- Purpose: inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the day's plan
- *Not* a status meeting for managers
- *Not* a problem-solving meeting

---
## Pitfalls and Patterns

![standup_pitfalls](svg/courses/practices/agile-and-scrum/04_standups/standup_pitfalls.svg)

---
## Why It Exists

- Developers coordinate: what one person did affects what another does
- Surface impediments early so they can be removed
- Renew commitment to the Sprint Goal
- Catch small misalignments before they become large ones
- Replace "who's doing what?" emails with a 15-minute conversation

---
## The Classic Three Questions

- What did I do yesterday?
- What will I do today?
- What's blocking me?
- Each developer takes a turn answering
- Time-box: usually a minute or two each

---
## Why the Three Questions Often Fail

- Developers report status to nobody in particular
- The Scrum Master writes it all down for absent managers — meeting becomes a status report
- "Working on the same thing as yesterday" repeats for days
- Nobody actually listens to anybody else
- The team reaches the end having coordinated nothing

---
## A Better Format: Walk the Board

- Open the Sprint Backlog on a screen or whiteboard
- Walk each item from "Done" leftward toward "To Do"
- For each in-progress item: who's on it, what's the next step, anything blocking
- Items not started yet: skip
- The conversation centres on the *work*, not on individuals

---
## Another Format: Focus on Blockers

- Skip the "yesterday" / "today" theatre
- Just go around: "anything blocking you right now?"
- Resolve quickly or take it offline with a small group
- Add a short discussion of any backlog item the team needs to align on
- Works well for mature teams with high trust

---
## Time-Boxing

- 15 minutes total — *for the meeting*, not per person
- If you have 10 developers, that's 90 seconds each — that's the design
- A timer on the screen helps
- "Take it offline" is the magic phrase — invoke it ruthlessly
- Discussions that need to happen will continue *after* the standup with the right people

---
## Common Anti-Patterns

- Manager runs the standup and asks pointed questions
- People show up late or leave early
- Same person speaks for 5 minutes every day
- Architectural debates that should be a separate meeting
- The whole team standing while one developer types updates into a tool

---
## Manager Attendance

- Managers can attend but not run, ask, or interrupt
- Their job is to listen for impediments to remove
- If a manager's presence makes developers censor themselves, they shouldn't be there
- Some teams ban management presence; that's overreach in the other direction
- Trust is the variable; adjust accordingly

---
## When To Skip a Standup

- The day before a holiday and there's nothing new
- The whole team is in another meeting and skipping is cheaper than juggling
- Skipping is a *team* decision, not an individual one
- Skipping more than once a sprint is a smell — why doesn't the team value it?
- The honest answer is usually "we don't get value from it" — fix the format

---
## Remote and Distributed Standups

- Same rules apply: 15 minutes, focused on the work, blockers surfaced
- Cameras on for the conversation, off if bandwidth is a problem
- One person at a time — chaos in audio is worse than in person
- Asynchronous standups (Slack thread): work for some teams, not others
- Time zones: pick the slot that minimises misery, rotate if necessary

---
## Asynchronous Standups

- Each developer posts in a channel by a deadline
- Format: blockers, plans, recent progress
- Pros: respects deep work, accommodates time zones
- Cons: no real conversation, blockers can sit unread
- Hybrid: async post + a 15-minute sync only if anyone has a topic to discuss

---
## Standup Health Check

- Are blockers surfacing within a day of appearing?
- Are people listening, or scrolling their phones?
- Does anyone leave the standup with a clearer plan than they came in with?
- Does the team adjust the day based on what was said?
- If the answer to all four is no, the standup isn't working — change the format
