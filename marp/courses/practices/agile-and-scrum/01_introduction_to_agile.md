## A Short History

- Software projects in the 1990s ran on Waterfall: spec, design, build, test, ship
- Many of them shipped late, over budget, or never
- 2001: 17 practitioners met in Snowbird, Utah and wrote a one-page manifesto
- The manifesto did not invent these ideas — it organised what was already working
- 25 years later, "Agile" is the default vocabulary in the industry

---

## The Agile Manifesto

- We have come to value:
- **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools
- **Working software** over comprehensive documentation
- **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
- **Responding to change** over following a plan
- The right side has value; the left side has more value

---

## Reading the Manifesto Carefully

- "Over" is not "instead of" — both sides matter
- The right side ships unshippable software on time
- The left side is what makes the difference between a 6-month project and an 18-month one
- Teams that quote the manifesto often forget the right-hand side exists
- Documentation, plans, and contracts still matter — they just stop being the goal

---

## The Twelve Principles, Distilled

- Deliver working software early and often
- Welcome changing requirements, even late in development
- Business and developers work together daily
- Build projects around motivated individuals; trust them
- Face-to-face conversation is the most efficient communication
- Working software is the primary measure of progress
- Sustainable pace; reflect and adjust regularly

## Waterfall vs Agile

![waterfall_vs_agile](svg/courses/practices/agile-and-scrum/01_introduction_to_agile/waterfall_vs_agile.svg)

---

## What Waterfall Optimises For

- Predictability: a clear plan, a clear price, a clear date
- Suits projects where the requirements really are stable
- Building a bridge: requirements don't change after the design phase
- The model isn't *wrong* — it's wrong for *most software*
- Software requirements change because we don't know what we want until we see something

---

## What Agile Optimises For

- Adaptability: small steps, frequent course-corrections
- Suits projects where requirements emerge from interaction
- Most product software fits here — even ones the customer thinks are stable
- Trade-off: harder to give a fixed-date, fixed-scope estimate up front
- "We don't know exactly what we'll have built in six months" — and that's a feature

---

## Benefits

- Faster feedback &#8594; fewer expensive surprises
- Smaller increments &#8594; easier to test, easier to roll back
- Closer customer involvement &#8594; we build things people want
- Team autonomy &#8594; better motivation, better problem-solving
- Continuous improvement &#8594; the team gets better at its job, not just the product

---

## Costs

- Up-front planning is harder to sell to traditional stakeholders
- Continuous interaction with the business is *required*, not optional
- Heavy-handed Agile theatre (rituals without principles) burns morale
- Distributed teams across time zones make daily collaboration harder
- Requires real cultural change; you can't just install Jira and call it Agile

---

## Common Agile Flavours

- **Scrum**: timeboxed sprints, defined roles and events. Most popular.
- **Kanban**: continuous flow, work-in-progress limits. Often paired with Scrum (Scrumban).
- **Extreme Programming (XP)**: engineering-heavy — TDD, pair programming, refactoring
- **Lean**: "eliminate waste", borrowed from Toyota production system
- **SAFe / LeSS / Nexus**: scaling Agile across many teams (with varying success)

---

## What Agile Is Not

- A magic productivity boost
- An excuse to skip planning ("we're agile, we'll figure it out")
- A reason to skip documentation
- A way to extract more work from developers ("velocity must go up")
- A guarantee that the project will succeed

---

## What Agile *Is*

- A bias toward learning faster than the competition
- A bias toward smaller, safer changes
- A bias toward humans solving problems together
- A bias toward "build less, but build the right thing"
- A bet that *adaptability* beats *prediction* on most software work
