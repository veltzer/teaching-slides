## Machine Learning, Plainly

- Algorithms that *learn patterns from data* instead of being explicitly programmed
- Trained on historical examples; make predictions on new data
- Classic algorithms: linear regression, decision trees, random forests
- Performance scales with data quantity and quality
- Most "AI" in business today is supervised ML on tabular data

---
## Three Kinds of ML

- **Supervised**: learn from labelled examples ("this is spam, this isn't")
- **Unsupervised**: find structure in unlabelled data (clustering, anomaly detection)
- **Reinforcement**: learn by trial and reward (rare in business apps)
- Most useful business problems are supervised
- Picking the right kind matters more than picking the right algorithm

---
## Deep Learning

- A specific kind of ML using neural networks with many layers
- Excels at unstructured data: images, audio, text
- Requires lots of compute and lots of data
- Underlies modern image recognition, speech, translation, LLMs
- Overkill for many tabular ML problems where simpler models do better

---
## Why Deep Learning Took Over

- Data: the internet provided massive labelled and unlabelled corpora
- Compute: GPUs made training tractable
- Algorithms: better architectures (transformers, ResNets)
- Tooling: PyTorch, TensorFlow lowered the barrier
- The result: unstructured-data problems became solvable

---
## NLP Today

- Translation: superhuman in many language pairs
- Sentiment analysis, classification, entity extraction: very capable
- Summarisation and Q&A: useful but unreliable on edges
- Code generation: dramatically improved with LLMs
- Hallucination is real — verify outputs before automating decisions

---
## Large Language Models (LLMs)

- GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama, Mistral
- Trained on huge text corpora; can converse, write, analyse
- Useful for: drafting, summarising, classification, extraction
- Risky for: anything requiring perfect facts, long-term memory, real-time data
- Plug-in tools, retrieval (RAG), and agents extend the basic capabilities

---
## Where AI Pays Off

- High-volume decisions where small accuracy gains compound (fraud, ads, recommendations)
- Unstructured data at scale (image classification, text triage)
- Personalisation (each user gets a slightly different experience)
- Search and discovery (better matches between query and content)
- Automation of repetitive analysis (categorisation, basic Q&A)

---
## Where AI Doesn't Pay Off

- Decisions made fewer than once a day
- Decisions that already work well via simple rules
- Cases where mistakes are expensive and unrecoverable
- Areas without enough quality training data
- Anything where explainability is a hard requirement and the model is a black box

## Case Study: Fraud Detection

- Classic ML success
- Banks process billions of transactions; tiny accuracy gain = real money
- Models retrain regularly as fraud patterns evolve
- Combined with rule-based filters and human review
- Quietly running in the background of every credit card swipe

---
## Case Study: Recommendation Systems

- Netflix, Spotify, Amazon, YouTube — all personalised by ML
- Drives massive engagement and retention
- Data hungry; cold-start is hard
- Feedback loops: recommendations shape behaviour, which retrains the model
- Done well, invisible; done poorly, frustrating

---
## Case Study: Chatbots Gone Wrong

- Microsoft Tay (2016): learned to be racist within a day
- Air Canada chatbot (2024): made up a refund policy; airline held responsible
- LLM-powered support bots that hallucinate prices, policies, refunds
- Lesson: AI without guardrails is a liability
- Test for failure modes, not just happy paths

---
## Build vs Buy vs API

- **API** (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google): fastest to start, ongoing cost, vendor lock-in
- **Buy** (existing AI products): faster than building, narrower scope
- **Build**: full control, requires team, only worth it for differentiation
- Most companies should *use* AI, not *build* foundational models
- The right level of customisation depends on your specific problem

---
## The Talent Question

- A senior ML engineer is expensive and rare
- A senior data engineer is more impactful for most companies
- Without good data plumbing, models can't be deployed reliably
- Hire for the bottleneck, not the buzzword
- Many "AI" hires end up doing data engineering for 6 months first

---
## Ethics and Bias

- Models inherit the biases in their training data
- Recidivism predictions, hiring screens — all have notorious failures
- Audit models before deployment, especially in high-stakes domains
- Document training data, model decisions, monitoring
- Regulators (EU AI Act, sector-specific rules) are catching up

---
## A Decision Frame

- Is the problem repetitive at scale?
- Do we have (or can we get) quality labelled data?
- Can we tolerate some error rate?
- Is "no result" or "uncertain" a valid output?
- Can we monitor and roll back if things go wrong?
- If yes to all five: AI may help. Otherwise: probably not.

---
## Common Mistakes

- "We need to use AI" without a clear problem
- Underinvesting in data quality before modelling
- Deploying without monitoring or rollback
- Treating models as forever-correct after shipping
- Confusing demo accuracy with production accuracy (data drift)
