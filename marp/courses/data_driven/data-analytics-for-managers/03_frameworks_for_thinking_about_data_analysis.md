---

## What a Framework Is

- A *mental model* for organising metrics into a coherent story
- Replaces "what should we measure?" with "where does this metric fit?"
- Lets a team align on a small number of numbers that matter
- Without one: dashboards balloon and meaning thins out
- With one: even disagreements happen at the right level

---

## Why Frameworks Help

- They force prioritisation: 5 numbers, not 50
- They surface *causal* relationships between metrics
- They make trade-offs explicit: improving X likely hurts Y
- They survive personnel changes — institutional memory
- New team members can learn the model quickly

---

## How Frameworks Hurt

- A wrong framework hides important things
- Frameworks become orthodoxy; "we don't measure X because the framework says so"
- Cargo-culting: copy-pasting AARRR onto a B2B sales product
- Replacing thinking with structure
- Use them as *scaffolding*, not as *gospel*

---

## The Four Aspects

- **What** to measure: which metrics matter
- **How** to measure: definitions, sources, formulas
- **Why** to measure: business rationale per metric
- **When** to act: thresholds and triggers
- A complete framework answers all four

---

## AARRR (Pirate Metrics)

- **A**cquisition — how do users find you?
- **A**ctivation — first meaningful interaction
- **R**etention — do they come back?
- **R**eferral — do they bring others?
- **R**evenue — do they pay?
- A funnel for product growth, in five letters

## When AARRR Fits

- Consumer products with self-serve sign-up
- Free-tier or freemium models
- High-volume, lower-touch transactions
- Where a "user funnel" is a real thing
- Less appropriate for enterprise sales (long cycles, single accounts)

---

## HEART (UI / UX)

- **H**appiness — sentiment, satisfaction
- **E**ngagement — depth of use
- **A**doption — new users
- **R**etention — returning users
- **T**ask success — efficient, error-free use
- For UX teams, complements AARRR

---

## North Star Metric

- *One* metric that captures whether your product is working
- Spotify: "time spent listening". Airbnb: "nights booked"
- Forces clarity; no hiding behind 12 dashboards
- Tracked obsessively; tied to compensation; reviewed weekly
- Hard to pick; harder to commit to

---

## Lagging vs Leading

- **Lagging** indicators: what already happened (revenue, churn)
- **Leading** indicators: what predicts what's next (signups, NPS, support tickets)
- Both matter; teams often track only lagging
- Leading indicators give you time to act
- A good dashboard mixes both

## OKRs and Metrics

- Objectives and Key Results: a goal-setting framework
- "Improve onboarding" (objective) — "Day-7 retention from 30% to 45%" (key result)
- Force quantitative thinking
- Cap at 3-5 KRs per objective
- The *act of writing* sharpens thinking; the resulting numbers are secondary

---

## Choosing a Framework

- Match the *shape* of your business
- E-commerce: AARRR + cohort analysis
- SaaS: ARR + MRR + churn + NPS
- B2B sales: pipeline stages, win rate, deal size, cycle time
- Game studio: DAU/MAU, ARPU, day-N retention
- Don't borrow another industry's framework whole

---

## Adapting Over Time

- The right framework today is wrong in 18 months
- Product evolves &#8594; metrics evolve
- Refresh quarterly; review yearly
- Retire metrics no one acts on
- Add metrics that capture new user behaviours

---

## Implementing a Framework

- Document each metric: definition, source, owner, threshold
- Build dashboards *after* the document is signed off
- Train the team to *read* the dashboards
- Hold a weekly review until the team naturally uses the metrics
- Without rituals, even the best framework dies

---

## Common Mistakes

- Adopting a framework without adapting it
- Tracking 50 metrics instead of 5
- Pretty dashboards nobody actions
- Using frameworks to justify decisions already made
- Ignoring leading indicators because they're noisy
